Hungary White Card 2026: The Digital Nomad Visa Explained

Think of the White Card as a trial run for Central Europe, not an immigration strategy. It's cheap, it's fast, and it has a hard ceiling that Spain's and Portugal's digital nomad visas don't share.

What the White Card is

Hungary's White Card is a residence permit for remote workers employed by, or contracting for, a foreign company. It lets you live in Budapest or elsewhere in Hungary while working entirely for clients or an employer outside the country.

Requirements and numbers

FactorDetail
Income requirement€3,000/month from a foreign employer or client base
Processing time30–90 days
Duration1 year + 1 year extension (2 years total maximum)
Work allowedForeign employer/clients only — no local Hungarian employment
Path to PRNone — cannot lead to permanent residency
Path to citizenshipNone, directly
Cost~€100

The hard 2-year ceiling

This is the detail that separates the White Card from Spain's and Portugal's digital nomad visas: it caps out at 2 years total, full stop. There's no renewal path beyond that, and the visa itself doesn't convert into permanent residency or citizenship — if you want to stay longer, you need to switch to an entirely different Hungarian visa track before your White Card runs out.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Easy, fast entry point into Central Europe
  • Access to Hungary's 15% flat personal income tax for qualifying residents
  • Full Schengen travel while resident

Cons

  • No path to permanent residency or citizenship on this visa alone, ever
  • Hard 2-year total maximum, no exceptions
  • A spouse can't be added as a dependent on the White Card itself — they need a separate visa route

Who this actually fits

The White Card makes the most sense as exactly what it is: a 1-2 year trial run in Central Europe for remote workers who want low cost of living and a flat tax rate, not people planning a long-term European base. If you want a digital nomad visa that eventually leads somewhere — permanent residency, citizenship — look at Spain's or Portugal's DNV instead.
