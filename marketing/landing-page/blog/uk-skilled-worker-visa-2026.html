UK Skilled Worker Visa 2026: Complete Guide for Americans

The most common UK work visa route runs through one thing: a licensed employer willing to sponsor you. Get that, clear the salary bar, and it's a straightforward 5-year path to settlement.

What the Skilled Worker Visa is

This is the UK's main employer-sponsored work visa. Unlike the Ancestry Visa, it has nothing to do with family history — you need a job offer from a UK employer that holds a valid sponsor license, in a role that meets the skill and salary requirements.

Requirements and numbers

FactorDetail
Income requirement£38,700+/year (lower "going rate" thresholds apply for shortage occupations)
Processing time3–6 weeks
Duration5 years
Work allowedYes, tied to your sponsoring employer
Path to PR5 years to Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)
Path to citizenship6 years total
Cost£625–1,500 + Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS), ~£3,105/year

Before you apply: confirm the sponsor license

This visa only works if your employer already holds a UK sponsor license — it isn't something you can arrange yourself, and not every UK company has one. Before you get excited about a job offer, ask the employer directly whether they're a licensed sponsor for the Skilled Worker route. This single check prevents the most common source of wasted time in this whole process.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The most common and well-trodden UK work-visa route
  • Your spouse or partner gets an open work permit and can work for any employer
  • Direct 5-year path to Indefinite Leave to Remain

Cons

  • You're tied to your specific sponsor — changing jobs means finding a new sponsoring employer and reapplying
  • The salary floor rose sharply to £38,700 in 2024, up significantly from prior years
  • The IHS compounds over a 5-year visa to roughly £15,500 for one applicant, on top of the visa fee itself
