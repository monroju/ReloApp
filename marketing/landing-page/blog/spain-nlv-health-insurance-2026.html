Spain Non-Lucrative Visa Health Insurance: What Consulates Accept in 2026

Health insurance is the document that sends the most NLV files back. The rules are strict, and most international or travel policies miss at least one of them. Here is what the consulates actually write, and how to check a policy before you pay for a year of it.

The short version. Your policy must be (1) from an insurer authorised to operate in Spain, (2) full cover, the same as Spain's public health system, (3) with no copays, no deductibles, no reimbursement and no waiting periods, and (4) valid for at least the first year. Wording differs a little between consulates, so read yours too.

What the consulates say

Each Spanish consulate publishes its own NLV checklist. Three of them, in their own words:

Melbourne (the most detailed)

"Comprehensive, non-reimbursable health insurance with benefits similar to those granted by the National Health System, arranged with an insurance company authorized to operate in Spain that covers, at a minimum, the entire validity period of the initial non-lucrative temporary residence authorization (one year from its issuance), 100% of the medical, hospital and extra-hospital expenses ... for a minimum amount of 30,000 euros ... and is exempt from co-payments, reimbursements or waiting periods, contracted with an insurer that must be registered in the list of insurance and reinsurance entities of the Directorate General of Insurance and Pension Funds."

Source: Consulate General of Spain in Melbourne, NLV requirements (PDF)

Miami

"The insurance must cover all risks normally covered for nationals ... The health insurance must not have a deficiency, copayment, or coverage limit."

Miami also says the cover must be kept for the whole authorisation, and that it means health insurance, not travel insurance. Source: Consulate General of Spain in Miami, non-working residence visa

London

"The insurance policy must cover all the risks insured by Spain's public health system."

London wants the original certificate from an insurer authorised to operate in Spain, and also accepts a receipt of S1 form registration with Spanish Social Security (useful if you draw a state pension from an EU country). Source: Consulate General of Spain in London, non-lucrative residence visa

What gets a policy rejected

  • Travel or travel medical insurance. It covers emergencies on a trip, not everything the public system covers.
  • Reimbursement plans. If you pay the doctor and claim it back later, that is a reimbursement model. Melbourne rules it out by name.
  • Copays and deductibles. Any amount you pay yourself before or during treatment.
  • Waiting periods (carencias), where some treatments are only covered after a few months.
  • Caps and gaps, such as a yearly limit, or missing outpatient or hospital cover.
  • An insurer not authorised in Spain. A big international brand is not enough on its own: the insurer has to be on the DGSFP register.
  • Less than a year of cover, or a policy that starts after your visa would.

Many international "expat" and digital nomad plans are good insurance but still fail here, usually on reimbursement, deductibles or waiting periods. That does not make them bad, just wrong for this file.

Your options

1. A Spanish insurer's visa plan

The big Spanish insurers sell plans made for residence visas, usually without copays or waiting periods. Sanitas, Adeslas, DKV and Asisa are the names consulates see most often. Most of their documents are in Spanish, and a local broker can help you compare them.

2. An English-speaking expat plan that is registered in Spain

Feather's expat health insurance for Spain is sold in English and underwritten by an insurer registered with the DGSFP (code L1497). Feather says the plan has no copays, deductibles or waiting periods, and gives you a stamped, signed certificate that lists the compliance points. In its own FAQ, Feather lists three limits: a known pre-existing condition from the 6 months before the start date, annual caps on some extras (for example pain-relief dentistry and massages), and a 6-month wait for routine pregnancy checkups and elective childbirth. 3. S1 registration (EU state pensioners)

If an EU country pays your state pension, you may be able to use the S1 form instead of private cover. London accepts it for the NLV. Check with your consulate first.

After you arrive

Keep the policy active for as long as your residence authorisation lasts. A lapse can cost you your renewal, which asks for proof of cover again. Put the renewal date in your calendar the day you sign.
