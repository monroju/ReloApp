Argentina Visa Rentista 2026: Complete Guide for Americans

A lower income bar than almost anything in Europe, and one of the fastest legal paths to citizenship in the world — two years of residency, then you're eligible to naturalize. Here's how the Visa Rentista actually works.

What the Visa Rentista is

Argentina's Visa Rentista is for people who can support themselves on passive income — dividends, rental income, investment returns — without working locally. It's the standard route for Americans who want South American residency without the higher income bars common in Western Europe.

Requirements and numbers

FactorDetail
Income requirement~$2,000/month USD passive income
Processing time2–4 months

Cost$200 + ~$300 in administrative costs

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Lower income bar than most comparable European passive-income visas
  • One of the fastest residency-to-citizenship timelines anywhere — Argentine law allows naturalization after just 2 years of legal residency
  • Argentine citizenship also opens up Mercosur-linked travel and residency benefits across much of South America

Cons

  • Argentine peso volatility affects your local cost-of-living math even though the USD income requirement itself doesn't change
  • No local work allowed under this visa category
  • Annual renewal is required in the first year, so paperwork discipline matters early on

The Visa Pensionado alternative

If your income comes specifically from a lifetime pension rather than general passive income, Argentina also offers a Visa Pensionado, which doesn't carry a fixed minimum-amount requirement the way the Rentista does. GoThere's catalog doesn't yet have a full breakdown of this route — if a pension is your main income source, check Argentina's official immigration site directly for current Pensionado requirements.
