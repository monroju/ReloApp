Italy Elective Residency Visa 2026: Complete Guide for Americans

Roughly €31,000 a year in passive income is the lowest bar for long-term residency anywhere in Western Europe on GoThere's catalog. The trade-off: you can never work, and you need Italian property lined up before you even apply.

What the Elective Residency Visa is

Italy's Elective Residency Visa (Visto per Residenza Elettiva) is built for people who can support themselves entirely on passive income — pensions, dividends, rental income, investment returns — without working in Italy at all. It's the classic route for American retirees who want a long-term Italian base without going through the ancestry process.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Lowest passive-income threshold of any Western European long-stay visa in our catalog
  • Family members can be included on the application
  • Genuine path to EU permanent residency and, eventually, citizenship

Cons

  • No work allowed, ever, for the life of the visa
  • You generally need to own or have a long-term lease on Italian property before you can apply — this isn't optional paperwork, it's a prerequisite
  • Local Questura (immigration police) processing for your residence permit after arrival can be slow and inconsistent city to city

The property requirement, and why it matters

Unlike Spain's Non-Lucrative Visa or Portugal's D7, Italy generally wants proof of housing lined up in Italy before you submit your consular application, not after arrival. Budget time and money for this step before you start the visa clock — it's a common reason applications stall.
