Leaving the US: 6 realistic paths abroad

Whatever happens on November 3, a lot of Americans are quietly asking the same question: could I actually live somewhere else? Here are the six paths that most often work for US citizens, with the real numbers from official sources. No hype, no "move tomorrow" promises.

I'm an American who moved to Málaga. Most people who look into this stop at "I don't know where to start." So start here: find the path that matches how you earn money, check the number, then work the checklist.

The six paths
  1. 🇪🇸 Spain Non-Lucrative Visa
  2. 🇪🇸 Spain Digital Nomad Visa
  3. 🇵🇹 Portugal D7 or D8
  4. 🇲🇽 Mexico Temporary Residency
  5. 🇮🇪🇮🇹🇨🇦 Citizenship by descent
  6. 🇩🇪 Germany EU Blue Card

1 🇪🇸 Spain Non-Lucrative Visa

Retirees and anyone living on savings, pensions or investments. You can't work in Spain on it.

€2,400 a month in passive income (€28,800 a year), plus €600 a month for each family member.

Basis400% of IPREM (€600/month in 2026), +100% IPREM per dependent
WorkNot allowed in Spain
Path to permanent residency5 years
Citizenship10 years of residence

Official source: Spain Ministry of Inclusion. Checked 2026-10-08. Rules change; confirm with your consulate.

2 🇪🇸 Spain Digital Nomad Visa

Remote workers employed by (or freelancing for) companies outside Spain.

Basis200% of Spain's 2026 minimum wage (€1,221/month, 14 payments), per the official FAQ
WorkRemote, for companies or clients outside Spain
Path to permanent residency5 years

3 🇵🇹 Portugal D7 or D8

D7 for passive income (pension, rentals, dividends). D8 for remote workers. The lowest income bar in Western Europe.

D7: €920 a month in passive income (+€460 per extra adult, +€276 per child). D8: €3,680 a month in remote-work income.

BasisPortugal's 2026 minimum wage is €920/month. D7 = 100% of it; D8 = 4 times it
WorkAllowed (D7 includes local jobs)
Path to permanent residency5 years

Official source: DGERT: 2026 minimum wage · AIMA: means of subsistence. Checked 2026-10-08. Rules change; confirm with your consulate.

4 🇲🇽 Mexico Temporary Residency

The fastest, closest option. Proves income or savings; the exact amount depends on your consulate.

About $4,400 to $4,630 a month in income (last 6 months), or $73,000 to $78,000 in savings (last 12 months), depending on the consulate.

Basis680 days (income) or 11,460 days (savings) of Mexico's UMA, converted by each consulate
ExampleLas Vegas consulate, 2026: $4,630/month or $78,025
WorkNeeds a separate permission
Path to permanent residencyAfter 4 years of temporary residency

Official source: Mexican Consulate Las Vegas (2026) · INEGI: UMA. Checked 2026-10-08. Rules change; confirm with your consulate.

5 🇮🇪🇮🇹🇨🇦 Citizenship by descent

If a parent or grandparent was born in Ireland or Italy, or you have Canadian ancestry, you may already have a second citizenship to claim. No income test.

No income test. Ireland: a grandparent born in Ireland (fee €278). Italy: since the 2025 reform, generally a parent or grandparent who held only Italian citizenship. Canada: Bill C-3 took effect December 15, 2025.

IrelandForeign Births Register, about 12 months, €270 + €8 postage for adults
ItalyLaw 74/2025: born abroad with another citizenship = Italian only if a parent or grandparent held exclusively Italian citizenship, or a parent lived in Italy 2 years before your birth (or you applied by March 27, 2025). An EU Court of Justice referral is pending.
CanadaPeople born abroad beyond the first generation before Dec 15, 2025 became citizens. After that, the Canadian parent needs 1,095 days in Canada.

Official source: Ireland DFA · Italian Consulate New York · IRCC: Bill C-3. Checked 2026-10-08. Rules change; confirm with your consulate.

6 🇩🇪 Germany EU Blue Card

Degree holders with a job offer. The fastest route to EU permanent residency on this list.

A job offer paying at least €50,700 a year (lower in shortage fields such as IT, engineering and healthcare), plus a recognized degree.

WorkYes, for the employer that sponsors you
Path to permanent residency21 months with B1 German, 27 months with A1

Before any of the six: the US-side paperwork

  1. Check your passport. Valid at least 6 months beyond your planned stay, with blank pages.
  2. Order an FBI Identity History Summary. Most residence visas want a recent federal background check.
  3. Plan apostilles early. State documents get apostilled by that state's Secretary of State; federal ones by the US Department of State. This can take weeks.
  4. Order long-form birth and marriage certificates. Certified copies, often with apostille and a sworn translation.
  5. Find your consulate. You apply at the consulate that covers the US state where you legally live, not the one with the shortest wait.
  6. Price health insurance. Most visas require private coverage with no co-pays for at least the first year.
  7. Talk to a US tax professional. You keep filing US taxes from abroad. Ask about FBAR and the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion before you move money.
