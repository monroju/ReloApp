Best Passive-Income & Retiree Visas for Americans (2026)

If you can prove steady income without working locally, these are the visas that let you settle abroad. Portugal's D7 has the lowest income bar in the EU; Spain's NLV forbids all local work; Italy's Elective Residency is the cheapest passive route in Western Europe. Here they are side by side.

Each visa in detail

🇪🇸 Non-Lucrative Visa — Spain

Income: ~€2,400/mo passive (4× IPREM, +25% per dependent) · Processing: Up to 3 months (legal max) · Cost: $153 consular fee (US) + lawyer optional
Path to PR: 5 years · Citizenship: 10y (2y Latin Am/Sephardic)

Pros

  • No employer needed
  • Family included (+25% IPREM per dependent)
  • Path to EU passport

Trade-offs

  • No work in Spain at all
  • Renewal scrutinizes time outside Spain
  • Costly health insurance required

🇵🇹 D7 Passive Income Visa — Portugal

Income: ~€920/mo (€11,040/yr) passive · Processing: 4-6 months · Cost: €110 visa + lawyer ~€1,500
Path to PR: 5 years · Citizenship: 10 years (7 for CPLP/EU)

Pros

  • Lowest income bar in Europe
  • Permanent residence after 5 years
  • Work allowed in Portugal

Trade-offs

  • Slow AIMA processing
  • 183-day/yr presence required
  • NHR tax regime ended Mar 2024

🇮🇹 Elective Residency Visa — Italy

Income: ~€31,000/yr passive per applicant (consulate guidance) · Processing: Up to 90 days · Cost: €116 visa + ~€120 permit + lawyer optional
Path to PR: 5 years · Citizenship: 10 years

Pros

  • Lowest passive bar in Western Europe
  • Family included
  • Path to EU citizenship

Trade-offs

  • No work allowed, ever
  • Must own/rent property in Italy
  • Slow Questura processing

🇮🇪 Stamp 0 — Retiree Visa — Ireland

Income: €50,000/yr passive income per person · Processing: ~4 months · Cost: €300 IRP registration + accountant certification
Path to PR: No direct path · Citizenship: Disputed, check with ISD

Pros

  • Live in EU member state
  • Low presence requirement
  • No work obligation

Trade-offs

  • High income bar
  • No Stamp 4 route; citizenship counting disputed
  • Cannot work — ever

🇲🇽 Temporary Resident (Economic Solvency) — Mexico

Income: ~$4,250/mo (6mo) or ~$72k savings (12mo) · Processing: ~10 business days (consulate) + INM card · Cost: $56 consulate + ~$600 INM card
Path to PR: 4yr → Permanent Resident · Citizenship: 5y from first resident card

Pros

  • Quick processing
  • Easy bank deposit proof
  • No language test

Trade-offs

  • Cannot work for Mexican employer
  • Income threshold rises yearly
  • Must enter within 6mo

🇲🇽 Permanent Resident Visa — Mexico

Income: ~$7,100/mo pension or 4yr Temp held · Processing: ~10 business days (consulate) + INM card · Cost: $56 consulate + ~$730 INM card
Path to PR: Immediate · Citizenship: 5 years

Pros

  • No renewal forever
  • Full work rights immediately
  • Bring vehicles in tax-free

Trade-offs

  • Higher income bar than Temp
  • Loses status with 5y absence
  • Not all consulates issue direct

🇦🇷 Visa Rentista (Passive Income) — Argentina

Income: ~$2,000/mo USD passive · Processing: 2-4 months · Cost: $200 + ~$300 admin
Path to PR: 2 years · Citizenship: 2 years from PR

Pros

  • Low income bar vs Europe
  • Fast 2yr path to PR + citizenship
  • Mercosur passport access

Trade-offs

  • Peso volatility
  • Cannot work locally
  • Annual renewal required first year
