Moving from the US to Portugal in 2026

Most Americans move to Portugal on one of two routes: the D7 Visa for retirees and people living on passive income, which needs €920 (≈ $1,029)/month; or the D8 Digital Nomad Visa for remote workers for employers or clients outside Portugal, which needs €3,680 (≈ $4,117)/month.

Either way, Portuguese citizenship takes 10 years of legal residence.

Portugal D7 Visa: who it suits and what it takes

For retirees and people living on passive income. Official name: Residence visa for retirees, religious workers and persons living on own income (D7).

Income required
€920 (≈ $1,029)/month (100% of the national minimum wage (RMMG), Portaria 1563/2007)[official source]
Per dependent
+50% RMMG (EUR 460/mo) per additional adult; +30% RMMG (EUR 276/mo) per child under 18 or dependent adult child[official source]
Decision time
up to 60 days[official source]
Government fees
National (residence) visa fee: €110 (≈ $123) [official source]
First permit
24 months; renewable for successive 3-year periods[official source]
Can you work?
Yes[official source]
Permanent residence
after 5 years[official source]
Citizenship
after 10 years of residence[official source]

How to apply for the D7 Visa from the US

  1. Gather the residence visa documents. Proof of retirement pension, or of passive income from movable/immovable property, intellectual property or financial investments, plus proof the income is available in Portugal; passport, 2 photos, application form, travel/health insurance, apostilled criminal record certificate, proof of means of subsistence.[source]
  2. Book and submit the application at VFS Global (US). Portuguese consulates in the US process visa applications exclusively through VFS Global (New York centre: 128 East 32nd Street). Apply at the centre for the consulate covering your US state.[source]
  3. Pay the national visa fee (EUR 110). Exemptions include children under 6. A VFS service fee is charged on top (amount not verified).[source]
  4. Wait for the consular decision (legal limit 60 days).[source]
  5. Enter Portugal within the visa's 4-month validity. The residence visa is valid for two entries and four months, during which you must apply to AIMA for a residence permit.[source]
  6. Attend AIMA and apply for the residence permit. In person at an AIMA Loja by appointment (online platform for visa holders being rolled out), with passport, valid visa, address declaration and proof of means; Modelo 1 form.[source]
  7. Receive the temporary residence permit. Valid 2 years from issue, renewable for successive 3-year periods.[source]
Legal basis and notes
  • Income: 2026 RMMG = EUR 920 (Decreto-Lei 139/2025 of 29 Dec 2025, stated on vistos.mne.gov.pt and DGERT https://www.dgert.gov.pt/retribuicao-minima-mensal-garantida-para-2026). Applicant: 100% x 920 = 920/mo; 920 x 12 = 11,040/yr. Spouse/second adult: 50% x 920 = 460/mo. Each child under 18 or dependent adult child: 30% x 920 = 276/mo. Example couple + 2 children: 920 + 460 + 276 + 276 = 1,932/mo. Percentages from Portaria 1563/2007 art. 2 n.2 as stated on vistos.mne.gov.pt and aima.gov.pt (the AIMA page still shows the 2025 figure of EUR 870). Amount is net of social security contributions. The official page gives no annual figure; 11,040 is arithmetic.
  • Savings: No savings amount is stated on official pages for retirees or passive income: they must prove the income and its availability in Portugal. A 12-month RMMG balance (EUR 11,040) is commonly reported as consular practice by secondary sources, but this is unverified; on vistos.mne.gov.pt the 12-month rule is stated only for investors, researchers, religious workers and students.
  • Decision time: Official consular decision period for residence visas is 60 days 'except in circumstances stipulated by law' (vistos.mne.gov.pt Prazos). The Consulate General in New York states a minimum processing time of 4 to 8 weeks for national visas (https://novaiorque.consuladoportugal.mne.gov.pt/pt/assuntos-consulares/vistos). No official minimum, so min is null. After arrival, Lei 23/2007 art. 82 n.5 (version amended by Lei 62/2026 of 10 September) sets 90 days, extendable by 30, for AIMA to decide the residence permit (consolidated text read on pgdlisboa.pt, https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?artigo_id=920A0083&nid=920&tabela=leis&pagina=1&ficha=1&so_miolo=&nversao=). Real-world AIMA delays for appointments and permit cards are widely reported and often exceed these legal periods; no official backlog figure found.
  • Duration: initialMonths refers to the temporary residence permit issued by AIMA (Lei 23/2007 art. 75 n.1): 2 years from issue. The consular residence visa itself is valid 4 months, two entries (https://vistos.mne.gov.pt/pt/vistos-nacionais/informacao-geral/tipo-de-visto). The permit may be cancelled for absence over 6 consecutive or 8 non-consecutive months during its validity (art. 85 n.2).
  • Work: Lei 23/2007 art. 83 n.1 b) and c): a residence permit holder may do salaried and self-employed work without special authorisation. Source is the consolidated text on pgdlisboa.pt (Lisbon Regional Prosecutor's Office), version including Lei 62/2026.
  • Permanent residence: Permanent residence after holding a temporary residence permit for at least 5 years, plus basic Portuguese (e.g. A2 PLA certificate), means of subsistence, accommodation, tax/social security compliance and no conviction over 1 year in the last 5 years. Legal basis: Lei 23/2007 art. 80 with art. 64 and Decreto Regulamentar 84/2007 (art. 80 n.1 a) confirmed in the consolidated text including Lei 62/2026).
  • Citizenship: 10 years of legal residence for nationals of countries outside the CPLP and EU (US citizens), per Lei 37/81 art. 6 n.1 b) as amended by Lei Organica n.1/2026 of 18 May (approved by Parliament 1 Apr 2026, promulgated by President Seguro 3 May 2026, published DR 1st series n.95 on 18 May 2026, in force the next day, 19 May 2026, per its art. 8). CPLP and EU nationals: 7 years. Counting: art. 15 n.1 counts residence held under any title, visa or permit under the foreigners law; art. 15 n.3 sums all legal residence periods, continuous or not, provided they fall within a maximum window of 12 years for non-CPLP/non-EU nationals (9 for CPLP/EU, 6 for stateless). The old art. 15 n.4 is revoked (art. 5); secondary sources describe it as the 2024 rule that counted time from the residence permit application, so time spent waiting on AIMA before a permit is granted no longer counts (inference from secondary reporting; the revoked text itself was not read). Transitional rule (art. 7 n.2): applications pending on 19 May 2026 are decided under the previous law (5 years). New requirements also include a Portuguese language/culture test and knowledge of rights, duties and state organisation (art. 6 n.1 c, d). A first version was partly struck down by the Constitutional Court in Dec 2025 (Acordao 1133/2025, per secondary sources); a separate decree on loss of nationality was reported as still under Constitutional Court review. No official source found of any later change up to Oct 2026.
What we could not verify

Unverified: VFS Global US service fee and USD amount of the EUR 110 visa fee (VFS site blocked automated access); AIMA residence permit fee; whether US consulates require a 12-month savings balance or a Portuguese bank account; real AIMA backlog times. Conflict: AIMA meios de subsistencia page still shows the 2025 minimum wage (EUR 870); vistos.mne.gov.pt shows 2026 EUR 920. Old app fee EUR 90 is outdated (now EUR 110). Citizenship is 10 years for US nationals under Lei Organica 1/2026; applications filed before 19 May 2026 stay under the old 5-year rule. Constitutional Court ruling number from secondary sources only. Work rights article read via pgdlisboa.pt consolidated text, not the DR consolidated page.

Portugal D8 Digital Nomad Visa: who it suits and what it takes

For remote workers for employers or clients outside Portugal. Official name: Residence visa for remote professional activity (digital nomad, D8).

Income required
€3,680 (≈ $4,117)/month (4x the national minimum wage (RMMG), average of the last 3 months)[official source]
Decision time
up to 60 days[official source]
Government fees
National (residence) visa fee: €110 (≈ $123) [official source]
First permit
24 months; renewable for successive 3-year periods[official source]
Can you work?
Restricted[official source]
Permanent residence
after 5 years[official source]
Citizenship
after 10 years of residence[official source]

How to apply for the D8 Digital Nomad Visa from the US

  1. Gather the residence visa documents. Employment contract or employer declaration, or service contracts with entities abroad; proof of average monthly income over the last 3 months of at least 4x RMMG; proof of tax residence; passport, 2 photos, application form, travel/health insurance, apostilled criminal record certificate.[source]
  2. Book and submit the application at VFS Global (US). Portuguese consulates in the US process visa applications exclusively through VFS Global (New York centre: 128 East 32nd Street). Apply at the centre for the consulate covering your US state.[source]
  3. Pay the national visa fee (EUR 110). Exemptions include children under 6. A VFS service fee is charged on top (amount not verified).[source]
  4. Wait for the consular decision (legal limit 60 days).[source]
  5. Enter Portugal within the visa's 4-month validity. The residence visa is valid for two entries and four months, during which you must apply to AIMA for a residence permit.[source]
  6. Attend AIMA and apply for the residence permit. In person at an AIMA Loja by appointment (online platform for visa holders being rolled out), with passport, valid visa, address declaration and proof of means; Modelo 1 form.[source]
  7. Receive the temporary residence permit. Valid 2 years from issue, renewable for successive 3-year periods.[source]
Legal basis and notes
  • Income: Official requirement: proof of average monthly income over the last 3 months from subordinate or independent work of at least four RMMG (Lei 23/2007 art. 61-B). 2026 RMMG = EUR 920 (Decreto-Lei 139/2025). 4 x 920 = 3,680/mo; 3,680 x 12 = 44,160/yr (arithmetic, not stated on the page).
  • Savings: No savings requirement appears on the official D8 residence visa checklist; means of subsistence are assessed from the work or service contracts. The '12x minimum wage savings' figure seen online is not on official pages and is unverified. Dependents: no official D8 add-on is stated; the general Portaria 1563/2007 percentages (+50% adult, +30% child) may be applied to accompanying family, unverified.
  • Decision time: Official consular decision period for residence visas is 60 days 'except in circumstances stipulated by law' (vistos.mne.gov.pt Prazos). The Consulate General in New York states a minimum processing time of 4 to 8 weeks for national visas (https://novaiorque.consuladoportugal.mne.gov.pt/pt/assuntos-consulares/vistos). No official minimum, so min is null. Real-world AIMA delays for appointments and permit cards are widely reported and often exceed these legal periods; no official backlog figure found.
  • Duration: initialMonths refers to the AIMA residence permit: 2 years from issue, renewable for successive 3-year periods. The consular residence visa itself is valid 4 months, two entries (https://vistos.mne.gov.pt/pt/vistos-nacionais/informacao-geral/tipo-de-visto). A separate temporary-stay remote work visa (under 1 year) exists and is not this record.
  • Work: The visa is granted for remote work for employers or clients domiciled outside Portugal (Lei 23/2007 art. 61-B). Once the residence permit is issued, art. 83 n.1 b) and c) give permit holders the general right to salaried and self-employed work, 'without prejudice to special provisions'. No official page says whether Portuguese-sourced work affects renewal of a D8-based permit, so marked restricted. Consolidated text read on pgdlisboa.pt.

Legal basis: Lei 23/2007 art. 80 with art. 64 and Decreto Regulamentar 84/2007 (art. 80 n.1 a) confirmed in the consolidated text including Lei 62/2026).

Questions Americans ask about moving to Portugal

How much income do I need for the Portugal D7 Visa?

€920 (≈ $1,029)/month (100% of the national minimum wage (RMMG), Portaria 1563/2007), according to the official source, last checked 2026-10-26.

How long does the Portugal D7 Visa take?

The official decision period is up to 60 days. Real-world timelines are often longer once you add document gathering and appointments.

Can I work in Portugal on the D7 Visa?

Yes.

How much income do I need for the Portugal D8 Digital Nomad Visa?

€3,680 (≈ $4,117)/month (4x the national minimum wage (RMMG), average of the last 3 months), according to the official source, last checked 2026-10-26.

How long does the Portugal D8 Digital Nomad Visa take?

The official decision period is up to 60 days. Real-world timelines are often longer once you add document gathering and appointments.

Can I work in Portugal on the D8 Digital Nomad Visa?

Restricted.

How long until I can become a Portuguese citizen?

Counting legal residence: 10 years on the D7 Visa; 10 years on the D8 Digital Nomad Visa. Citizenship by descent, if you qualify, can be much faster.
