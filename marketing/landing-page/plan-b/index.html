Plan B: a second citizenship you may already have a claim to

If a parent or grandparent came from Ireland, Italy, Germany, Poland or Hungary, you may be able to claim that citizenship by descent. It is paperwork, not a move: you can start collecting records now and decide later whether to use it.

Options, not exits. Applications often take one to three years or more, so the slow part is worth starting early.

Irish Citizenship via Foreign Births Register

You can register through a grandparent born on the island of Ireland, or through a parent who was an Irish citizen when you were born. A great-grandparent line works only if your parent was entered on the Foreign Births Register before you were born. If a parent was born on the island of Ireland, you are already Irish and apply for a passport instead. source, checked 2026-11-06

What you get: Full Irish + EU citizenship + passport. Right to live and work anywhere in the EU/EEA + UK.

Main documents (the app builds the full list for each person in your line):

  • Foreign Births Register online application (printed, signed and witnessed)
  • Witness sign off: stamp on the form, 2 signed photos, certified copy of your ID
  • Birth certificate (original civil, long form showing parents)
  • Irish-born grandparent's civil birth certificate (original, showing parents)
  • Marriage certificate or change of name document (original civil)
  • Certified photocopy of current photo ID (passport, driver's license or national ID)
  • Death certificate (original civil)
  • Two separate original proofs of address

Plus 3 more.

Fees

  • Foreign Births Register online application (DFA): EUR 278 for adults (EUR 270 registration and certificate plus EUR 8 postage and handling); EUR 153 under 18. source, checked 2026-11-06
  • Irish civil certificates, Republic of Ireland (General Register Office): EUR 20 per certificate. A research copy of an entry is EUR 5 and is not a certificate. source, checked 2026-11-06
  • Northern Ireland civil certificates (GRONI, nidirect): GBP 15 per certificate; extra copies of the same certificate ordered together GBP 8 each; postage to the rest of the world from GBP 1.20. source, checked 2026-11-06

Italian Citizenship by Descent

Since 28 March 2025, if you were born abroad and hold another citizenship, Italy recognizes you only if a parent or grandparent has, or had at death, only Italian citizenship, or a parent lived in Italy for 2 continuous years after becoming Italian and before your birth (Law 91/1992 art. 3-bis). Great-grandparent lines are closed unless your recognition was granted, or your application was filed at an appointment set, by 23:59 Rome time on 27 March 2025. Ruling 63/2026 rejected the challenge. In July 2026 ordinance 147/2026 sent a separate question on this rule to the EU Court of Justice; the rule stays in force while that is pending. Through a mother, the line passes only to children born after 1 January 1948. source, checked 2026-11-06

What you get: Full Italian + EU citizenship + passport. Right to live and work anywhere in the EU/EEA.

Main documents (the app builds the full list for each person in your line):

  • Application for Italian citizenship recognition (notarized, with apostille)
  • Consulate Forms 1 and 2 (notarized, with apostille)
  • Form 3 signed by your living Italian parent or grandparent, or Form 4 signed by you if they died (notarized, with apostille)
  • Italian parent's or grandparent's birth extract (estratto dell'atto di nascita) from the comune
  • Birth certificate (long form, with apostille and Italian translation)
  • Italian ancestor's marriage certificate
  • Marriage certificate (long form, with apostille and Italian translation)
  • Death certificate (long form, with apostille and Italian translation)

Plus 7 more.

Fees

  • Consulate appointment booking (Prenot@mi): EUR 600 per adult recognition application (consular fee table). New York takes payment by money order or cashier's check to the Consulate General of Italy in New York; the fee is non-refundable. source, checked 2026-11-06

German Citizenship by Descent (Feststellung, Art. 116, StAG §5 and §15)

There are three routes. (1) Feststellung: you may already be German by descent; the BVA checks it and issues a Staatsangehörigkeitsausweis. US missions ask for proof of descent back to an ancestor born in 1914 or earlier, with one Anlage V per generation, so the line often reaches a great grandparent or further. (2) Art. 116 (2) GG and § 15 StAG: naturalization for descendants of people persecuted between 1933 and 1945; all descendants qualify under Art. 116 (2). (3) § 5 StAG declaration: for people born after 23 May 1949 who did not get German citizenship from a German parent, for example children of a German mother who lost it by marrying a foreigner, and their descendants. The § 5 right can only be used within ten years after the law took effect (BVA: possible since 20 August 2021). Documents needed for only one route are marked with that route. source, checked 2026-11-06

What you get: Full German + EU citizenship + passport. Right to live and work anywhere in the EU/EEA. Does NOT require renouncing US citizenship.

Main documents (the app builds the full list for each person in your line):

  • BVA application form: Antrag F (Feststellung), Antrag A (Art. 116), Antrag E15 (§ 15) or Vordruck EER (§ 5)
  • Ancestor supplement: Anlage V (Feststellung), Annex VA (Art. 116) or Anlage AV (§ 15, § 5), one per generation
  • Current passport or ID (photo and data pages), certified copy
  • Birth certificate (long form, showing parents)
  • Marriage certificate
  • Divorce decree (final), certified translation
  • German ancestor's birth or marriage record (Geburtsurkunde, Heiratsurkunde) from the Standesamt
  • Proof of the ancestor's German citizenship (old German passport, Heimatschein, citizenship certificate, naturalization certificate, registration or military records)

Plus 5 more.

Fees

  • Download BVA forms and send the application: Feststellung: EUR 51 if approved; EUR 25 to 51 if refused; EUR 38 if withdrawn after work starts. Art. 116 (2) GG and § 15 StAG: free. § 5 declaration: not verified. source, checked 2026-11-06
  • Get certified copies (US notary or German mission): Free at German missions for citizenship applications. source, checked 2026-11-06

Polish Citizenship Confirmation

Confirmation is for people who already hold Polish citizenship by descent and want an official decision. The national application asks for data on you, your parents and your grandparents, and the US consulate asks for documents on the Polish origin of parents or grandparents. Older lines (great grandparents) may be possible, but the official pages do not state a generation limit, so GoThere plans for a grandparent line. Older rules on losing Polish citizenship can break a line; check them for your family. source, checked 2026-11-06

What you get: Full Polish + EU citizenship + passport.

Main documents (the app builds the full list for each person in your line):

  • Application for confirmation of Polish citizenship (wniosek o potwierdzenie posiadania lub utraty obywatelstwa polskiego)
  • Valid ID document, original shown for inspection (including pages with official notes)
  • Your full birth certificate (long form, with parents' names)
  • Birth certificate (long form) of each parent and grandparent in the line
  • Marriage certificate, divorce decree or spouse's death certificate (your marital status)
  • Marriage certificates in the line (and name change proof)
  • Polish civil record copy (odpis aktu urodzenia or małżeństwa) from the USC
  • Proof of the ancestor's Polish citizenship (old Polish passport, ID card, military book, other Polish documents)

Plus 2 more.

Fees

  • File the confirmation application through a Polish consulate in the US: USD 118 at a consulate in the US. PLN 277 if filed directly with a voivode office in Poland. source, checked 2026-11-06
  • Transcribe a US birth or marriage record into the Polish registry: USD 71 source, checked 2026-11-06

Hungarian Simplified Naturalisation (2011 law)

Simplified naturalization is open to a non-Hungarian whose ancestor was a Hungarian citizen, or who can show likely origin from Hungary. Ancestor records can come from parents, grandparents, great grandparents and further back, but the chain must be complete: every generation in between needs a record. On the maternal line, marriage records are needed to link maiden and married names. A relative already naturalized through the same ancestor can save you the ancestor records. Hungarian language is a legal condition: the officer who takes the application checks it in a short conversation; no exam certificate is needed, but you must understand and answer on your own at an intermediate level. source, checked 2026-11-06

What you get: Full Hungarian + EU citizenship + passport.

Main documents (the app builds the full list for each person in your line):

  • Hungarian language check at submission (no exam certificate)
  • Naturalization application form (Honosítási, visszahonosítási kérelem, Act LV of 1993 § 4 (3)), with handwritten CV
  • Valid photo ID (for example US passport), shown at submission
  • Your birth certificate, original or certified copy, with certified Hungarian translation
  • Data sheet to register your foreign birth (and marriage) in Hungary
  • Your marriage certificate, divorce decree or spouse's death certificate, with certified Hungarian translation
  • Civil birth, marriage or death certificates of parents, grandparents and great grandparents, with certified Hungarian translation
  • Proof of the ancestor's Hungarian citizenship (church or archive certificate with exact volume, page and entry, Hungarian passport, military book, work book, school certificate, naturalization or citizenship certificate)

Fees

  • Submit in person at a Hungarian consulate in the US: Free. Only translations made by the consulate are charged. source, checked 2026-11-06

Questions

Which countries pass citizenship down to Americans?

GoThere covers Ireland, Italy, Germany, Poland and Hungary. Each has its own rules on how far back the line can go, and the details on this page come from each country's official pages.

Do I have to move to get a second citizenship by descent?

No move is needed to apply through these routes. Most of the work is collecting birth, marriage and naturalization records for each person in your line, which is why it is worth starting early.

How long does it take?

Official review times where they are published: Ireland: about 12 months; Germany: 24 to 36 months. Gathering records adds months on top, depending on how fast each records office answers.

Can a great-grandparent count?

In Germany and Hungary, the line can run through a great-grandparent or further. In Ireland it works only if your parent was registered on the Foreign Births Register before you were born, and in Italy the 2025 law usually stops the line at a grandparent.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is information from official sources, dated. Rules and fees change, so check the linked official page before you apply.
